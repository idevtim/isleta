# Isleta 1.3.1

Two ways into Settings that no longer depend on the menu bar icon — the gear is on every open island
now, and Export Logs… is inside Settings itself — and a notification list that opens smoothly.

## Improved

- **The gear is on every open island.** It used to appear only when there was something else to
  switch to, so an island with music playing and nothing waiting had no way into Settings at all.
  Open any island and rest the pointer on it — the row along the bottom has the gear in it, the same
  as when notifications are being held.
- **Opening the notification list is smoother.** With a full list the island used to stutter as it
  grew to fit it. Only the rows you can see are built now, and most of that stutter is gone in both
  directions.
- **Export Logs… is in Settings ▸ About.** It is the one thing you are asked for in a bug report,
  and it used to live only behind the menu bar icon, which you can now hide. It writes the same
  single file, wherever you choose to save it — no notification text, track titles or file names in
  it.

## Requirements

macOS 26 or later, on Apple Silicon. Displays with a real notch get the island; a Mac with none gets
a synthesized island on the primary display.

## Known limitations

- **The battery is a snapshot, not a readout.** It is read once, when the device connects, and the
  island goes a few seconds later. It does not tick down while you wear them — macOS gives no signal
  when the level changes, and Isleta will not poll for one.
- **Third-party headphones may show no battery.** Most non-Apple devices do not report a level to
  macOS at all. They still get their moment in the notch, with no ring beside them.
- **A burst of notifications opens the island once for each.** The list is where the ones you missed
  go; grouping them as they arrive is a later release's job.
- **A timer started by Siri, Shortcuts or Control Center can take a few seconds to appear.** One
  started by hand in Clock is there immediately; macOS gives no signal for the others, and Isleta
  will not poll for them.
- **The keyboard backlight HUD can appear when the room light changes.** macOS adjusts the
  backlight for ambient light on its own, and does not say whether a change came from you or from
  the room. Isleta ignores the idle fade, which is the common case, but cannot tell the other two
  apart. Turn off “Adjust keyboard brightness in low light” if it bothers you.
- **Apple's own HUDs and banners are not replaced.** Isleta shows its own alongside them. For
  notifications the only way to hide Apple's banner is the one the system treats as you clearing the
  notification outright, which would empty your Notification Center as the price of a tidier screen.
  Not a trade Isleta will make for you.
- **The island sits above Mission Control.** In a real notch it covers nothing; a synthesized island
  can cover the centre of the space labels.
- **Playing music costs more than it should.** About 7.7% CPU against an internal 4% budget for an
  animating island, and a 250–290 MB footprint against a 60 MB one. It is not a leak: resident
  memory is flat and plateaus rather than climbing.
- **Bluetooth headphones cost something at idle.** With AirPods connected, Isleta sits at about 0.5%
  of a core with nothing on stage, against an internal 0.3% budget; with none connected it measures
  0.02%. It is under investigation and it is not the AirPods *feature* — switching that source off
  does not move the figure, and neither the quiet island nor the AirPods moment adds a timer of its
  own.
