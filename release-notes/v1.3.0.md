# Isleta 1.3.0

Clicking the island when nothing is playing now opens it, onto the two things it always has: the
notifications you missed, and Settings. AirPods say hello when you put them in. And notifications
wear the icon of the app that sent them, scroll, and open that app when you click one.

## New

- **Brightness HUDs, for the display and the keyboard.** Change either and the island shows the
  level, the same way it already did for volume — the glyph in one sliver, the bar in the other. It
  arrives alongside Apple's own HUD rather than replacing it.

  Both were listed as impossible in the last four releases. Neither is: macOS publishes both levels
  and announces every change, just not through the APIs everyone reaches for first. Isleta reads
  them without asking for any permission, and stays completely idle between changes.

- **An empty island opens instead of refusing.** Click the notch on a quiet Mac and the island
  opens onto two buttons — Notifications and Settings — and nothing else. It is as short as an open
  island can be, and it closes the way any other does.
- **Settings is one click from the notch.** No shortcut to remember and no menu bar icon needed,
  which matters most if you have hidden the icon.
- **AirPods show up in the notch when they connect.** Take them out of the case and the island
  shows the AirPods themselves beside a ring of how much charge is left, for a few seconds, then
  goes. It works for AirPods, AirPods Pro, AirPods Max, Beats, and any other Bluetooth headphones or
  speaker — the ones Apple makes get their own picture, everything else gets a generic pair.
  The ring turns amber below 20%, so a pair that will not last the afternoon says so.
  Isleta asks for Bluetooth access the first time it starts — that is what lets it hear a device
  arrive — and the whole thing can be switched off in Settings ▸ Sources.
- **Notifications wear the icon of the app that sent them.** Mail, Messages, Calendar, Slack —
  whatever posted it, in the island and in the list, in place of the bell. The bell is still what
  you get for an app Isleta cannot find on the disk.
- **Clicking a notification opens the app it came from.** It works the way clicking a banner does:
  the app comes forward, the row goes, and the island closes behind it. Your Notification Center
  copy is left alone — Isleta reads that list, it does not clear it for you.

## Improved

- **The notification list no longer disappears when you click something else.** Clicking back into
  the window you were working in used to take the list away mid-read. It now stays until you dismiss
  it — with **Clear All**, with the **✕** beside it, or with Escape.
- **Clear All empties the list and closes the island** in one go, rather than leaving you looking at
  "Nothing recent".
- **The Notifications button says how many are new**, so a quiet island still tells you whether
  anything happened while you were away.
- **The notification list scrolls.** All twenty it keeps are reachable now, not just the five that
  fit — two fingers on the notch move the list, and a slim indicator down the edge says where in it
  you are. While the list is open that flick scrolls rather than closing the island; Clear All, the
  ✕ and Escape are still the ways out.
- **A notification arriving while you are reading brings the list back to the top**, so you watch it
  land instead of finding the rows rearranged under you.

## Fixed

- **The island no longer reopens at the wrong size after reading notifications.** Closing the list
  and opening the island again could leave it as tall as the list had been, with far less in it.

- **Two ear pieces are reported as the lower of the two.** If one bud is at 50% and the other at
  100%, the ring shows 50 — the one that runs out first is the one worth knowing about, and it is
  what Apple's own menu shows.

## Requirements

macOS 26 or later, on Apple Silicon. Displays with a real notch get the island; a Mac with none gets
a synthesized island on the primary display.

## Known limitations

- **The battery is a snapshot, not a readout.** It is read once, when the device connects, and the
  island goes a few seconds later. It does not tick down while you wear them — macOS gives no signal
  when the level changes, and Isleta will not poll for one.
- **Third-party headphones may show no battery.** Most non-Apple devices do not report a level to
  macOS at all. They still get their moment in the notch, with no ring beside them.
- **A burst of notifications opens the island once for each.** The list is where the ones you missed
  go; grouping them as they arrive is a later release's job.
- **A timer started by Siri, Shortcuts or Control Center can take a few seconds to appear.** One
  started by hand in Clock is there immediately; macOS gives no signal for the others, and Isleta
  will not poll for them.
- **The keyboard backlight HUD can appear when the room light changes.** macOS adjusts the
  backlight for ambient light on its own, and does not say whether a change came from you or from
  the room. Isleta ignores the idle fade, which is the common case, but cannot tell the other two
  apart. Turn off “Adjust keyboard brightness in low light” if it bothers you.
- **Apple's own HUDs and banners are not replaced.** Isleta shows its own alongside them. For
  notifications the only way to hide Apple's banner is the one the system treats as you clearing the
  notification outright, which would empty your Notification Center as the price of a tidier screen.
  Not a trade Isleta will make for you.
- **The island sits above Mission Control.** In a real notch it covers nothing; a synthesized island
  can cover the centre of the space labels.
- **Playing music costs more than it should.** About 7.7% CPU against an internal 4% budget for an
  animating island, and a 250–290 MB footprint against a 60 MB one. It is not a leak: resident
  memory is flat and plateaus rather than climbing.
- **Bluetooth headphones cost something at idle.** With AirPods connected, Isleta sits at about 0.5%
  of a core with nothing on stage, against an internal 0.3% budget; with none connected it measures
  0.02%. It is under investigation and it is not the AirPods *feature* — switching that source off
  does not move the figure, and neither the quiet island nor the AirPods moment adds a timer of its
  own.
