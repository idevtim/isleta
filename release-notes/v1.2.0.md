# Isleta 1.2.0

The island holds two things at once, Clock timers count down beside the notch, and hovering an open
island reveals a row for switching between everything Isleta is holding.

## New

- **Clock timers appear in the island.** Start a timer in Clock and the countdown rides in the
  sliver beside the notch, drawn as Clock's own ring — orange while it runs, grey while it is
  paused. No permission is asked for and nothing is polled while no timer exists.
- **Music and a timer at the same time.** Music takes the left of the notch and a countdown the
  right, whichever of the two is holding the island open.
- **Hover an open island for the switcher.** A row of chips appears along the bottom listing
  everything Isleta is currently holding. Click one to bring it to the front — it stays there for
  half a minute, longer than a swipe holds it. Move the pointer away and the row goes.
- **Recent notifications, on the ✉ chip.** The last twenty that came through, including the ones
  that arrived while you were reading another and never made it to the island. Five fit; the rest
  are counted underneath. Held in memory only — never written to disk, never in an exported log,
  gone when Isleta quits.
- **The menu bar icon can be hidden**, in Settings › General. Your own shortcut still opens the
  island from any app, and the ⚙ chip in the switcher row is the way back into Settings.

## Improved

- **A finished timer does not take over the island.** It shows itself beside the notch the way a
  volume change does, and Clock's own notification is left to be the thing you click.
- **Long countdowns fit the sliver.** An hour and four minutes reads `1h04` rather than being
  clipped to `4:` above an ellipsis.
- **The island grows and settles for the switcher row** on one spring, in both directions, instead
  of snapping it in and out.

## Requirements

macOS 26 or later, on Apple Silicon. Displays with a real notch get the island; a Mac with none gets
a synthesized island on the primary display.

## Known limitations

- **A timer started by Siri, Shortcuts or Control Center can take a few seconds to appear.** One
  started by hand in Clock is there immediately; macOS gives no signal for the others, and Isleta
  will not poll for them.
- **Brightness has no HUD.** macOS offers no public way to read display brightness on Apple Silicon.
- **Apple's own HUDs are not replaced.** Isleta shows its own alongside them.
- **The island sits above Mission Control.** In a real notch it covers nothing; a synthesized island
  can cover the centre of the space labels.
- **A burst of notifications opens the island once for each.** The ✉ chip is where the ones you
  missed go; grouping them as they arrive is a later release's job.
- **Playing music costs more than it should.** About 7.7% CPU against an internal 4% budget for an
  animating island, and a 250–290 MB footprint against a 60 MB one. It is not a leak: resident
  memory is flat and plateaus rather than climbing. Idle is unaffected — around 0.04% with nothing
  on stage, and a timer counting down does not measurably change that.
