# Isleta 2.0.0

The notch became something you can browse. Three pages — your day, your music, the weather — turned
with a two-finger swipe, with everything that arrives on its own taking the stage in front of them.
Now in German, French and Spanish.

## New

- **Three pages, and a swipe between them.** Today, Music and Weather. Swipe two fingers across an
  open island to turn the page. They wrap, so there is no end to walk into.
- **Your day, in the notch.** What is next in your calendar, with a button that joins the meeting.
  Choose which calendars count in Settings.
- **The weather where you are.** Today's high and low, what it feels like, and the chance of rain
  for the next few days. Use your location, or type a city and pick it from the list.
- **When it's raining, it rains in the island.** The drops land on its bottom edge. Snow falls too,
  and both stop if you have asked macOS to reduce motion.
- **A music page that shows the whole track.** Long titles scroll and then settle, the album artwork
  lends the island its color, and the audio format shows as a small badge.
- **Up Next.** The queue your player is working through, the output device, favorite and playback
  speed. Double-click anything in the list to play it.
- **Drop files on the island to convert them.** Images, images to PDF, video, audio, Word, RTF,
  HTML, spreadsheets and presentations. Search the shelf, preview with Space, and look back through
  everything Isleta has made for you.
- **Drop audio or video to transcribe it.** The text lands beside the original. Nothing is uploaded
  and nothing is asked of you.
- **Copy Link** puts a shareable iCloud link on your clipboard. For files outside iCloud Drive it
  offers AirDrop instead.
- **Isleta can answer your volume and brightness keys.** Turn it on under Sources and the island
  replaces macOS's own HUD rather than sitting beside it. Volume and brightness are separate
  switches, both off until you turn them on.
- **A card on the lock screen** showing what is playing. It arrives when the Mac locks and leaves
  the moment you unlock it. Off until you turn it on.
- **AirPods and other Bluetooth devices announce themselves** when they connect, with the battery
  level if the device reports one.
- **Battery, calls and timers.** The charger going in and out, a low battery, Low Power Mode, a call
  in progress with how long it has been going, and timers you start in Clock.
- **A first run that asks once.** Eight short screens, five of them permissions — accessibility,
  music, calendar, weather and devices — each showing what the permission is for, and which button
  to press, before it asks. Skip any of them and the rest of Isleta still works.
- **A list of apps the island stays out of entirely**, for full-screen video or a presentation.
- **Keyboard shortcuts** for opening the island and showing your day, under General. Opening the
  island ships bound to ⌃⌥⌘I, because Isleta has no Dock icon and its menu bar item can be hidden;
  showing your day is unassigned until you assign it.
- **Quick actions in the menu bar** for whatever is on the island right now. The icon can be hidden;
  Quit and Open Setup Guide live in About as well.
- **German, French and Spanish**, following your Mac's language.

## Improved

- **Settings is a much smaller place.** Four panes instead of seven, and roughly a third as many
  controls. What went were the settings asking you a question Isleta can answer for itself.
- **The island is black in a real notch and glass where it floats**, and no longer asks you to
  choose. It was never a matter of taste, so it is no longer a setting.
- **The whole island moves as one.** Its width, height and corners travel together, so opening it
  reads as a single motion rather than two.
- **Reduce motion, reduce transparency and increase contrast are honored throughout.**

## Fixed

- **Settings opens on the desktop you are looking at**, rather than the one you last opened it on.
  Opening Settings now closes the island.
- **Playing music costs a fraction of what it did.** A playing track is now about as light as a
  quiet island.
- **Closing Up Next gives the island its height back.** The ✕ used to leave it tall.
- **AirPods connecting no longer hangs Isleta at launch.**

## Requirements

macOS 26 or later, on Apple Silicon. Displays with a real notch get the island; a Mac without one
gets a floating island on the main display.

## Known limitations

- **You cannot see who is calling.** macOS does not let apps outside Apple read that, or answer for
  you. Isleta shows that a call is happening and nothing more.
- **Weather needs your location, or a city you type.** Isleta asks for neither until you use it.
- **A connected device's battery is read once, when it connects.** It does not tick down as you wear
  them, and most non-Apple headphones report no level at all.
- **A timer started by Siri, Shortcuts or Control Center can take a few seconds to appear.** One
  started by hand in Clock is there immediately.
- **Replacing the system HUDs is off until you turn it on, and it needs Accessibility.** Quitting
  Isleta hands your keys straight back; if Isleta is force-quit, the system HUD stays out of the way
  until Isleta next starts. Brightness covers the built-in display only.
- **The island sits above Mission Control.** In a real notch it covers nothing; a floating island
  can sit over the middle of the space labels.
