# Isleta 2.4.0

The music card on the lock screen is made of glass now: your wallpaper reads through it and bends
around its edges. Everything on it is bigger, too, for the times you are reading it from across the
room.

## Improved

- **The lock screen player is glass rather than frosted.** The wallpaper behind it comes through and
  refracts at the corners, the way the rest of the system does it. It becomes a solid dark panel if
  you have asked for reduced transparency.
- **The lock screen player reads from further away.** The elapsed and remaining times are larger,
  the progress line is thicker and runs most of the width of the card, and the Lossless or Dolby
  Atmos mark beside the artist is easier to pick out.
- **The buttons on the lock screen player are spread across the card.** Shuffle and repeat sit at
  the ends rather than huddled in the middle, so each one is easier to aim at without a cursor to
  guide you.
- **The button under the pointer on the lock screen is obvious now.** It takes a brighter wash and a
  defined edge, so it still shows against a pale wallpaper coming through the glass.
- **The album's color stays on the equalizer and off the scrub bar.** In the island's player the
  bars beside the cover still take the record's color; the position bar is plain white again,
  because a colored bar reads as though the color means something.

## Requirements

macOS 26 or later, on Apple Silicon. Displays with a real notch get the island; a Mac without one
gets a floating island on the main display.

## Known limitations

- **The lean at the end of a range needs Accessibility.** Reaching the end of the volume or
  brightness no longer moves the island on its own, and the press after it is only visible to Isleta
  with that permission granted. The reading itself, and the bar filling and emptying, work either
  way.
- **A muted Mac's bar cannot be dragged.** It is drawn at nothing whatever the volume behind it is,
  so pressing it opens the island instead. Unmute first.
- **You cannot see who is calling.** macOS does not let apps outside Apple read that, or answer for
  you. Isleta shows that a call is happening and nothing more.
- **Weather needs your location, or a city you type.** Isleta asks for neither until you use it.
- **A connected device's battery is read once, when it connects.** It does not tick down as you wear
  them, and most non-Apple headphones report no level at all.
- **A timer started by Siri, Shortcuts or Control Center can take a few seconds to appear.** One
  started by hand in Clock is there immediately.
- **Replacing the system HUDs is on by default, and it needs Accessibility.** Two switches in
  Settings hand the keys back. Quitting Isleta hands them back too; if Isleta is force-quit, the
  system HUD stays out of the way until Isleta next starts. Brightness covers the built-in display
  only.
- **Apple's own banner still appears when headphones connect.** It is drawn by the menu bar rather
  than by the HUD helper Isleta can stand in for, so the island announces the connection alongside
  it rather than instead of it.
- **The island sits above Mission Control.** In a real notch it covers nothing; a floating island
  can sit over the middle of the space labels.
- **Today and tomorrow show five entries and then a count.** A busy pair of days says how many it
  could not fit rather than scrolling.
